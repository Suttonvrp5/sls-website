The Problem

House-hunting from another country rarely works.

Photos flatter. Listings go stale. Landlords disappear. Without someone on the ground, you're relying on strangers and guesswork for one of the biggest decisions of your move.

Stay Safe

Rental scams are common on Samui.

Fake owners and fake agents are a well-known problem on Koh Samui, and many newcomers have lost deposits to them. Searching from overseas makes you an easy target. Working with Samui Luxury Stays keeps you protected.

Warning signs

  • Pressure to pay a deposit before you have seen the villa
  • An owner who is always ‘abroad’ and cannot meet
  • Prices far below the market, or photos that do not match
  • Payment requested to a personal account or by crypto

How we protect you

  • We visit and inspect every property in person
  • We check the landlord’s identity and right to let
  • We review the lease and deposit terms before you pay
  • We never ask you to pay before a lease is agreed

Safe payment rules

  • Never pay for a place you have not seen
  • Pay only against a signed lease and a written receipt
  • Confirm the account name matches the owner or company
  • If in doubt, ask us before you send money

Before You Rent

What to know before you move.

Leases & deposits

  • Typical leases run 6 to 12 months; short-term and monthly rentals cost more
  • A refundable security deposit and one month in advance are common
  • Always sign a written lease and keep copies
  • Agree who pays for repairs, pool and garden care in writing

Utilities & running costs

  • Ask if electricity and water are billed at utility rates or marked up
  • Internet: confirm fibre availability at the address
  • Air-conditioning is the largest electricity cost
  • Check the water source and pressure

Immigration & paperwork

  • Your landlord must report foreign tenants to Thai immigration (TM30)
  • Keep copies of your passport, visa and lease
  • Visa and work-permit questions are best put to a licensed immigration adviser

Getting around

  • Most areas are not walkable: plan for a car or scooter
  • An International Driving Permit is needed to drive legally
  • Taxis and ride-hailing exist but are costly; ask us for trusted drivers

Seasons & weather

  • Hot and dry from roughly February to August
  • Samui’s rainy season is typically October to December
  • Check roof, drainage and pool-area condition before you sign

Healthcare & daily life

  • Private hospitals on the island offer English-speaking care
  • Supermarkets, international schools and gyms cluster in the north and east
  • Health insurance is strongly recommended

This guide is general information only and is not legal or immigration advice. Rules, costs and seasons can change; please confirm current requirements before you travel.
