Rental scams on Koh Samui: how to spot them and stay safe

Samui Luxury Stays7 min read

Almost everyone who has lived on Koh Samui for a while knows someone who lost money to a rental scam, or came close. Fake owners and fake agents are a well-known problem, and people searching from overseas are the easiest targets. The good news is that these scams follow a handful of patterns, and once you know them, they are easy to avoid.

Why rental scams are common here

Several things make the island an attractive hunting ground for scammers. Demand for long-term rentals is high, good properties go quickly, and many newcomers are searching from another country with no way to check what they are told. Listings are spread across social media groups, messaging apps and classified sites where anyone can post, and the pressure to act fast makes careful checking feel like a risk of losing the place.

The most common patterns

The listing that does not exist

Photos are copied from a real villa or from a hotel website and posted with a low price. When you show interest, you are told many people want it and asked to pay a deposit to hold it. The home is not available, and often the person does not own it.

The owner who is always abroad

You are told the owner lives overseas and cannot meet, show the property or take a video call, but an “agent” or “caretaker” will handle things once you send money. The story is designed to explain why you cannot check anything.

The agent who is not an agent

Someone presents themselves as an agent, shows you a genuine property, takes a deposit or fee, and then has no authority to let it. The property is real, but the person never had the right to rent it to you.

The double let

The same villa is rented to several people at once, each paying a deposit, or it is sublet by someone who has no permission from the real owner. You arrive to find you have no tenancy at all.

The fake proof of payment

A screenshot, a document or a bank slip is sent to show that someone else has already paid, to push you to pay quickly. These are easy to fake.

Red flags to take seriously

  • Pressure to pay a deposit before you have seen the property, in person or on a live video walkthrough
  • An owner or agent who refuses a live video call, or always has a reason why they cannot meet
  • A price far below similar homes in the same area
  • Photos that do not quite match, or that you can find elsewhere online
  • Payment requested to a personal account, by crypto, or through an unusual method
  • An account name that does not match the owner or a registered company
  • No written lease, or a reluctance to put terms in writing
  • A story that keeps changing, or messages designed to make you feel rushed

How to check a rental is genuine

  • See it yourself, or have someone you trust see it. A live video walkthrough, where the person shows you something you ask for, is far better than photos.
  • Ask for proof of the right to let. This can be the title deed or a written authority from the owner. Ask the person to show it, and check that the name matches the person you are dealing with.
  • Check the identity of the landlord or agent. A genuine one will not mind showing ID.
  • Search the photos online. If the same pictures appear on other sites under other names, walk away.
  • Check the details with others. Neighbours, local property managers and reputable agents often know who actually owns a property.
  • Read the lease before paying anything. Make sure it names the right parties, the right property and the right amounts.

Paying safely

  • Never pay for a place you have not seen
  • Pay only against a signed lease and a written receipt
  • Make sure the account name matches the owner or a registered company
  • Be wary of anyone who insists on cash, crypto or a personal account
  • Keep records of every message, receipt and payment

If you think you have been scammed

Act quickly. Contact your bank straight away to ask whether the payment can be stopped or traced, and keep every message, screenshot and receipt. Report the matter to the local police, or to the Tourist Police on 1155, and bring copies of everything. Recovery is never guaranteed, which is why prevention matters far more.

A genuine landlord is not offended by questions. If someone is, treat that as your answer.

In short

Rental scams on Koh Samui are common, but they are predictable. Never pay before you have seen the property, verify who you are dealing with, put everything in writing, and pay only against a signed lease and a receipt.
