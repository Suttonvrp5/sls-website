Preparing your villa for monsoon season: a practical checklist

Samui Luxury Stays6 min read

Koh Samui's monsoon season, typically running from October through December, is predictable in its timing and unpredictable in its intensity. The villas that come through it undamaged aren't usually the newest or most expensive ones — they're the ones where someone walked the property properly before the rain started, not after.

Roof and gutters

  • Clear gutters and downpipes of leaves and debris before the first heavy rain, not after a blockage causes an overflow
  • Check roof flashing and seals around any skylights, chimneys or roof-mounted equipment
  • Trim overhanging branches that could shed heavy debris onto the roof in high wind

Drainage and grounds

Poor drainage is the single most common cause of monsoon damage on Samui villas, and it's almost always preventable. Walk the property specifically looking for low points where water could pool against the building, check that garden drains and swales are clear, and confirm that any pump systems for low-lying areas are tested and working, not just installed.

Electrical and equipment

Outdoor electrical fittings, pool pump housings, and any equipment in low-lying plant rooms deserve particular attention. Confirm seals are intact, RCD/circuit breaker protection is working, and — for villas in flood-prone areas — that critical equipment is raised above likely water levels rather than sitting directly on the ground.

Doors, windows and seals

Wind-driven rain finds its way through gaps that are invisible in dry weather. Check door and window seals, particularly on any that face the prevailing weather direction, and confirm that any pool or garden furniture that could become a projectile in high wind has a proper storage plan.

By the time water is visibly coming through a ceiling, the actual failure usually happened weeks earlier, somewhere much smaller and much easier to have prevented.
Do this walk-through before the season starts, not during the first storm

A pre-monsoon inspection takes an afternoon. A monsoon repair, done reactively during a storm, usually takes considerably longer and costs considerably more.

In short

None of this is dramatic work — it's a checklist, not a renovation. But it's exactly the kind of unglamorous, easy-to-defer maintenance that separates villas that sail through monsoon season from villas that spend the following dry season being repaired.
