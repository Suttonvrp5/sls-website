Choosing a villa management company: the questions to ask before signing

Samui Luxury Stays7 min read

Almost every villa management company on Koh Samui presents itself the same way online: professional photos, warm language about care and trust, a tidy list of services. That similarity is exactly why the decision is hard — and exactly why the right questions matter more than the website.

Ask how many villas one manager actually handles

This single number tells you more than almost anything else. A manager personally responsible for forty villas cannot know any one of them the way a manager responsible for eight can. Ask directly, and ask what happens to your villa specifically if that manager is unavailable for a week.

Ask to see a real owner report, not a sample

Any company can describe transparent reporting. Ask to see an actual, anonymised report from a real villa — the same one an existing owner receives. It should show income, expenses, occupancy and maintenance activity in enough detail that you could reasonably audit it, not just a friendly summary paragraph.

Understand the fee structure completely

  • Is the management fee a flat rate or a percentage of rental income?
  • Are maintenance and repair costs marked up, and if so, by how much?
  • Are there separate fees for guest turnovers, laundry, or pool and garden care?
  • What happens to the fee structure if the villa isn't actively rented?

Ask who is actually on the island

Some companies that market heavily to overseas owners subcontract the actual on-the-ground work to third parties they don't directly employ. Ask plainly who is employed by the company itself, who lives on Samui, and who you would actually be speaking to in an emergency at 11pm on a Sunday.

The company that answers these questions specifically, with real numbers, is telling you something. The company that answers only in reassuring generalities is telling you something too.
Talk to a current owner if you can

A short conversation with someone who has actually used the company for a year or two will tell you more than any proposal document. Most reputable companies will happily arrange this — hesitation to do so is itself worth noting.

In short

Choosing a management company is one of the highest-leverage decisions an overseas owner makes, because it determines the daily reality of owning a home you can't personally check on. Ask specific questions, insist on specific answers, and treat vague reassurance as a signal rather than a comfort.
