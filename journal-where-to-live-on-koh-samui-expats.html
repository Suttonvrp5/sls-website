Where to live on Koh Samui as an expat: an area-by-area guide

Samui Luxury Stays8 min read

The most common question we hear from people planning a move is simply: where should I live? Koh Samui is small enough to drive around in a few hours, yet its neighbourhoods feel very different. The best area depends on your work, your family, your budget and how you like to spend your days. This guide walks through the island area by area, with the trade-offs that matter to someone moving here, not just visiting.

Start with how you will live, not where it looks nicest

Before comparing beaches, decide which of these matter most. They narrow the choice faster than any map:

  • Your daily commute to work, school or the gym
  • Distance from the airport, if you travel often
  • Beach access versus a hillside view, since sea-view homes are often up steep roads
  • Noise and nightlife, from lively to completely quiet
  • Whether you will drive. Many areas are not walkable, so a car or scooter is usually part of island life
  • Your budget. Rent varies widely between areas and between standards of home

Koh Samui area by area

  • Bophut & Fisherman’s Village (North coast): Lively, convenient and close to the airport, with restaurants, a walking street and a large expat community. A good first base if you want things on your doorstep.
  • Bang Rak (North-east): Close to the airport and the Big Buddha, with shops and cafes and a calm, local feel.
  • Plai Laem (North-east): Between Bang Rak and Choeng Mon, central and well served, and popular with long-stay residents.
  • Choeng Mon (North-east): A calm, upmarket bay with soft sand, cafes and villas. Quiet, with the airport close by.
  • Chaweng Noi (East coast): A smaller, quieter bay just north of Chaweng, with hillside homes and sea views.
  • Chaweng (East coast): The busiest beach area, with the most shops, restaurants, nightlife and services.
  • Lamai (South-east): A wide beach and a lively town centre, with a mix of budget homes and luxury villas.
  • Maenam & Ban Tai (North coast): A long, quieter beach with an evening market and a relaxed pace. Good value, and popular with families and long-term residents.
  • Nathon (West coast): The island’s main town and ferry port, with the hospital, government offices and lower prices. Not a beach resort.
  • Lipa Noi (West coast): Peaceful, green and local, with calm water, sunsets and a mix of villas and affordable homes.
  • Taling Ngam (South-west): Remote, tranquil hillside villas with sunset views, away from the airport and nightlife.
  • Na Muang (South coast): Inland-feeling south of the island near the waterfalls. Green, quiet and good value.

Matching the area to the person

Families

Families often look at the north and north-east, where the supermarkets, international schools and private hospital care are easiest to reach. Maenam, Bophut and Choeng Mon are popular for this reason.

Remote workers

Reliable fibre internet matters more than the beach. Ask for a speed test at the address, not just the area, and have a backup connection ready. Bophut, Chaweng and the north are well served.

Retirees and quiet seekers

Many people choose Choeng Mon, Maenam, Lipa Noi or Taling Ngam for calm and sunsets. If you pick a remote hillside, check how far you are from healthcare and shops.

Couples and singles who like a social scene

Fisherman’s Village, Chaweng and Lamai have the most restaurants, bars and activities within a short drive or walk.

Things the photos will not tell you

  • How steep or rough the access road is, especially in the rainy season
  • Noise from roads, bars or neighbours at night
  • Whether the home floods or has drainage problems after heavy rain
  • The real internet speed and mobile signal
  • How long the drive to the airport, a hospital or a supermarket actually takes
