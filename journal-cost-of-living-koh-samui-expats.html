Cost of living on Koh Samui for expats: what to budget for

Samui Luxury Stays7 min read

“How much does it cost to live on Koh Samui?” is the question everyone asks, and the honest answer is: it depends far more on how you choose to live than on the island itself. A simple, local lifestyle can cost much less than in most Western countries, while a villa with a pool, imported groceries and a car can feel as expensive as home. This guide explains what drives each cost, so you can build a realistic budget rather than rely on a single headline number.

Rent is the biggest and most variable cost

Housing will be your largest expense, and it varies enormously by area, size, standard and whether a home is furnished, has a pool, or has a sea view. A simple condo or small house inland costs a fraction of a beachfront villa. Rent also tends to be higher in the busy tourist months, and shorter leases cost more per month than 6 to 12-month leases. Our area guide and where to live guide show how neighbourhoods compare.

The other costs to budget for

  • Electricity. Air-conditioning is usually the biggest bill, so ask for recent bills before you sign, and ask whether the landlord charges utility rates or adds a mark-up
  • Water, internet and mobile. Usually modest, but confirm fibre internet at the address if you work remotely
  • Pool and garden care, if your home has them. Check who pays
  • Food. Local markets and street food are affordable, while imported and Western-brand groceries carry a real premium
  • Transport. Most people need a scooter or car. Include fuel, insurance, servicing and the right licence
  • Healthcare and insurance. Private care is good but not cheap, so international health insurance matters
  • Schooling. International schools are a major cost for families
  • Visas and paperwork. Allow for agent or legal fees, and for the cost of renewing visas
  • Setting up. One-off costs such as the security deposit, first month’s rent, furniture and kitchen items if the home is unfurnished

Three ways to live, three different budgets

Simple and local

A modest home away from the beach, local food, a scooter and few imports. This is where the island is at its most affordable.

Comfortable

A well-kept home in a convenient area, a mix of local and Western food, air-conditioning, a car or scooter and regular eating out. Most expats fall somewhere here.

Luxury

A villa with a pool and a view, imported groceries, a car, domestic help and private healthcare. Costs approach or exceed those of many Western cities.

Ways to keep costs under control

  • Rent first, buy later. Living on the island for a few months shows you what you really spend
  • Choose a home a little inland or higher up. Beachfront and sea views carry a premium
  • Ask for electricity bills and whether utilities are at cost
  • Negotiate for longer leases, which are often cheaper per month
  • Shop at local markets and eat where locals eat

Build your own budget

Write down each line above with your own lifestyle in mind, and add 10 to 15 percent for the things you did not expect. Then test it by asking what the numbers look like in the rainy season, when electricity use and repair costs can rise.

In short

Koh Samui can be as affordable or as expensive as you make it. Rent and lifestyle choices decide almost everything.
