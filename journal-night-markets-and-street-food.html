Night markets and street food: eating like a local on Koh Samui

Samui Luxury Stays7 min read

Some of the best meals on Koh Samui cost less than a coffee at a beach club. They are cooked on a charcoal grill or a roaring wok at a night market, handed over in a paper tray and eaten at a plastic table under strings of lights. For guests who want to taste the island as locals do, a market evening is the most rewarding hour of the week.

This guide covers the island's best-known night markets and walking streets, the dishes worth looking for and a few habits that make the experience easier. Markets change their days, hours and stalls from season to season, so please confirm details locally before you go. Your villa team can check on the day.

Fisherman's Village Walking Street, Bophut

The Friday walking street is perhaps the island's best-known market. On Friday evenings the main road through the old village is closed to traffic and fills with stalls, live music and the smell of grilled seafood. It is more of an event than a market: couples stroll, families gather and restaurants spill out onto the street.

  • What to expect: handmade jewellery and crafts, clothing, souvenirs, street snacks and a lively, relaxed crowd.
  • When: typically Friday evenings from around five or six, running until late. The village itself is pleasant on any evening of the week.
  • Tip: arrive before the sun sets to enjoy the village in the golden hour, then stay for the market.

Chaweng night markets

Chaweng has several evening markets and bazaars close to the beach road and the town centre. They are the most convenient option for visitors staying on the east coast, and the choice is wide, from souvenir stalls to food courts selling pad thai, fried rice and grilled skewers at modest prices. It is lively, busy and tourist-friendly, and a good place to try many things in small portions.

Lamai night market

Lamai's market has a more local feel, with a stronger emphasis on regional dishes and a relaxed pace. It does not run every night, so check which evenings it operates. Southern Thai cooking is the draw here, including spicy curries, grilled fish and fresh fruit, all at local prices.

Maenam's Monday market

On the north coast, Maenam's weekly market draws residents for family-recipe dishes and everyday shopping, with far fewer tourists than the more famous markets. If you are staying nearby, it is an easy and authentic option, and the vendors are often delighted when visitors show real interest in their food.

Nathon and Bangrak

Nathon, the island's main port town on the west coast, has an evening market known for good-value local food, with grilled items and regional specialities. Bangrak, on the north coast near the Big Buddha, is known for its seafood strip, where freshly grilled fish and shellfish are served with a view toward Koh Phangan. Both reward guests prepared to leave the main tourist routes.

Days and hours change

Markets on Samui open on particular days, close for festivals and rain and sometimes move. A quick message to your villa team or concierge on the day saves a wasted trip.

What to eat

The joy of a night market is variety. A good approach is to walk the whole length first, notice which stalls have the longest queues of locals and then order a little from several. Dishes worth looking for include:

  • Pad thai. Stir-fried rice noodles with egg, bean sprouts and peanuts, cooked to order in front of you.
  • Som tam. Green papaya salad pounded in a mortar. Specify how spicy you want it, because the default can be fierce.
  • Grilled seafood. Prawns, squid, fish and shellfish cooked over charcoal, often served with a punchy dipping sauce.
  • Southern Thai curries. Rich, fiery and fragrant, with ingredients like fresh turmeric and local herbs. Gaeng tai pla, a pungent fermented-fish curry, is for the adventurous.
  • Massaman and green curry. Milder, creamier options that suit most palates.
  • Tom yum goong. A hot and sour soup with prawns, lemongrass and lime leaf.
  • Mango sticky rice. A classic dessert of sweet coconut sticky rice with ripe mango, best in the fruit season.
  • Fresh fruit and coconut ice cream. A cooling end to the evening.

Etiquette and practical tips

  • Bring cash, in small notes. Most stalls do not take cards.
  • Ask about spice. Saying "mai phet" (not spicy) or "phet nit noi" (a little spicy) is appreciated and prevents surprises.
  • Look for busy stalls. High turnover usually means fresher food.
  • Be sensible with raw foods and ice, particularly at the start of a trip, and carry hand sanitiser.
  • Bargain politely at craft and souvenir stalls, but not at food stalls, where prices are fixed and already low.
  • Share tables and be patient. It is part of the experience, and most vendors are cheerful about it.
  • Arrange transport. Parking can be difficult and taxis scarce late at night, so a pre-booked driver is a comfort.

Cooking and eating like a local

If the market sparks an appetite for more, a Thai cooking class is a natural next step. Many include a morning market visit, where you learn to choose herbs, chillies and fresh produce, followed by a hands-on lesson and a meal. For something private, a chef can do the same at your villa. Our article on private chefs explains how, and our restaurant guide covers the island's sit-down options.

Markets for families

Night markets are generally relaxed and family-friendly, though they can be crowded and warm. Children often love the colourful snacks and the live cooking. Keep a close eye on little ones in busy areas and near hot grills, and go early in the evening if you prefer more space.

The short version

Visit at least one night market during your stay. The Friday walking street in Bophut is the most atmospheric, Chaweng is the most convenient, and Lamai, Maenam, Nathon and Bangrak offer more local flavour. Go hungry, bring cash, walk the whole market before ordering and share a little of everything.
