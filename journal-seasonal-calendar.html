A seasonal calendar for Koh Samui villa owners

Samui Luxury Stays7 min read

Koh Samui doesn't run on a single climate or a single demand curve — it has a rhythm. Owners who learn that rhythm make better decisions about when to rent at a premium, when to schedule disruptive work, and when to come and enjoy the villa themselves.

The island's weather is a little different from much of Thailand, with its heaviest rains arriving later in the year. Here's how the year tends to flow.

High season — roughly December to April

The driest, sunniest stretch and the peak of demand. Rates are at their highest, with the Christmas and New Year fortnight commanding the most of all. This is the time to rent rather than renovate — keep the villa booked and pristine, and avoid any work that would disrupt guests.

The shoulder months — May to August

Warm, mostly pleasant, with occasional rain and softer demand than peak. A good window for longer-stay guests, for catching up on maintenance between bookings, and often for the better-value family visits owners enjoy. Rates ease but the island is still lovely.

The green season — roughly September to November

Koh Samui's wettest period, peaking around October and November, with the lowest occupancy of the year. While it sounds like a downside, this is the most valuable window of all for owners: it's the time to undertake refurbishment, deep maintenance, and the disruptive projects that would be impossible during peak — so the villa emerges fresh and ready for the December rush.

The quiet season isn't dead time. It's when the smart work gets done, so the busy season runs flawlessly.

A rough owner's calendar

  • Before December: finish all refurbishment; deep-clean and stage the villa for peak.
  • December–April: maximise bookings; keep presentation immaculate; minimal disruption.
  • May–August: maintenance catch-up; longer lets; owner and family visits.
  • September–November: major projects, repainting, renovations; rigorous monsoon-readiness checks on roof, drains and seals.
Monsoon readiness matters

Heading into the green season, the roof, gutters, drainage and seals deserve close attention. An hour of checking before the rains is worth far more than the repair bill after them.

In short

Work with the island's rhythm rather than against it: rent hard in the dry peak, maintain and improve in the wet quiet, and time your own visits for the gentle shoulder months. A villa managed to the calendar is a villa that runs smoothly all year.
