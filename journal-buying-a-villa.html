Buying a villa in Koh Samui: what overseas owners should know

Samui Luxury Stays9 min read

Buying a villa on a beautiful island is an emotional decision as much as a financial one. The trick is to let the heart choose the place and the head handle everything else — because in Thailand, the everything else has some particular features worth understanding before you commit.

This is general guidance, not legal advice — you should always take qualified local legal counsel before buying. But knowing the landscape in advance makes those conversations far more productive.

Understand how ownership works

Thailand restricts foreign ownership of land, which surprises many first-time buyers. There are well-established routes that overseas owners use — long leasehold arrangements, ownership of the building separately from the land, and certain company structures — each with different implications for control, resale and cost. None of this is a reason not to buy; thousands of foreigners own villas here happily. But the structure you choose matters enormously, and it is the first thing to get right with a good lawyer.

Location is everything — and it's specific

"Koh Samui" is not one market. A beachfront villa in Choeng Mon behaves very differently from a hillside home in Bophut or a sunset-coast property in Lipa Noi — in price, in rental demand, in privacy, and in how the climate treats it. Consider proximity to the airport, the character of the nearest beach, the road access in heavy rain, and the outlook. A stunning view you can only reach by a road that floods in monsoon is a different proposition than it first appears.

Do the due diligence properly

  • Title and land documentation, verified independently
  • Access rights and shared roads — confirm them in writing
  • Utilities: water source, electricity supply, and internet reliability
  • The condition of the build, ideally with an independent survey
  • Any management or community fees, and what they cover
  • Permissions, if you intend to rent the villa out
The villas that disappoint are rarely the ones with the wrong view. They are the ones where the unglamorous checks were skipped.

Budget beyond the purchase price

The sticker price is the beginning. Plan for transfer costs and taxes, legal fees, any furnishing or refurbishment, and then the ongoing reality of ownership: management, maintenance, utilities, staff, pool and garden care, insurance, and a sensible reserve for the tropical climate's demands. A villa is a wonderful thing to own, but it is an asset that needs feeding.

Think about management before you buy, not after

The owners who settle in most smoothly are those who line up trusted local management as part of the purchase — so the villa is cared for from day one, even before they move in or take their first booking.

In short

Buying in Koh Samui is very achievable for overseas owners who go in with their eyes open: the right ownership structure, the right location for how you'll actually use the villa, thorough due diligence, and a realistic view of the full cost of ownership. Get those right, and you'll have made one of the more enjoyable investments of your life.
