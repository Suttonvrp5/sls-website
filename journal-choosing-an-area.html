Choosing the right part of Koh Samui: a villa owner's guide to the island's areas

Samui Luxury Stays9 min read

Ask ten people on Koh Samui which part of the island is best and you will get ten answers, most of them delivered with total confidence. The truth is that "best" depends entirely on what the villa is for: a family holiday home, a rental that needs to fill its calendar, a quiet retreat, or a long-term residence. Each coast of the island suits a different purpose.

This guide walks around the island the way a first-time owner might, describing the character of each area, who it tends to suit, and the trade-offs that don't always make it into a brochure. It is a general orientation rather than a ranking, and conditions change from year to year, so treat it as a starting point for your own visits.

Start with the question, not the map

Before comparing beaches, it helps to be clear about the job the villa has to do. Three questions narrow the field quickly:

  • Will it be rented, used personally, or both? A villa meant to earn needs to sit where guests actually want to stay. A villa meant for the owner can afford to be more remote.
  • Who is the likely guest or resident? Families, couples, groups of friends and remote workers all weigh location differently.
  • How much do you value convenience versus calm? On Samui these two often pull in opposite directions, and the right balance is personal.

The north coast: Bophut, Maenam and Choeng Mon

The north is the island's most popular base for villa owners, and for good reason. It combines a relaxed pace with easy access to the airport, restaurants and the island's main ring road.

Bophut is home to Fisherman's Village, a lively old waterfront of restaurants, boutiques and bars, with a weekly walking street that draws visitors from across the island. Villas in the hills above Bophut tend to enjoy sea views toward Koh Phangan, and guests can reach dining and shopping in minutes. The compromise is a busier atmosphere and more traffic than the quieter coasts.

Maenam sits just to the west and has a noticeably calmer feel. Its long, gently curving beach is quieter than Chaweng or Bophut, and the area has a more local, village character with a good range of everyday amenities. Hillside villas here often offer generous views and privacy, and many owners find Maenam a strong balance of calm and convenience.

Choeng Mon, on the northeast corner, is known for its sheltered bay and relaxed, upmarket feel. It is close to the airport, which is a genuine advantage for owners who travel often and for guests who want to be settled within minutes of landing. Choeng Mon tends to attract guests who want a refined, peaceful stay without being far from the action.

The east coast: Chaweng and Lamai

Chaweng is the island's most energetic area, with the longest beach, the widest choice of restaurants and bars, and by far the most nightlife. For some guests that energy is the whole point. For others it is exactly what they want to avoid. Villas in Chaweng can perform well in rentals because demand is strong and consistent, but owners should be realistic that the setting is lively and the neighbours are close.

Lamai is the island's second major resort area, with a slightly more relaxed and less polished feel than Chaweng. It appeals to guests who want beach life, good value and a mix of local and international dining. The surrounding hills and southern coast offer plenty of quieter pockets for villas that want more seclusion.

Plai Laem, between Bophut and Chaweng, is quieter again and best known for its large Buddha statue and temple. It is a convenient central location with lower tourist density, which suits guests who want to move around the island easily.

The west and south: sunsets and seclusion

The island's west and southwest coasts are quieter and much less developed. This is where you find the long, unhurried evenings and some of the island's best sunsets, often with a sense of space that is hard to find on the busier eastern side.

Areas such as Lipa Noi and Taling Ngam attract owners looking for privacy, dramatic views and a slower rhythm of life. The trade-off is distance: restaurants, shops and the airport are further away, and the quieter setting is not for every guest. For owners who value calm above all else, or who plan to live on the island rather than rent short-term, the west and south deserve serious consideration.

Beachfront, hillside or somewhere in between

Within any area, the type of plot matters almost as much as the area itself.

  • Beachfront villas command a premium and are enormously appealing, but they also bear the full force of salt air, storms and sometimes beach erosion, so upkeep costs are typically higher.
  • Hillside villas offer views, breezes and privacy, and can feel exceptional at sunrise or sunset. Access roads, steepness and drainage deserve careful inspection, particularly after heavy rain.
  • Flat, inland villas are usually more affordable and easier to reach, and can work well for families who value a garden over a view.
Visit in the season you are least expecting

An area that feels magical in March can feel very different during the rainy months. If possible, visit twice, once in high season and once in the green season, before committing to a location.

Practical factors that rarely make the brochure

Beyond atmosphere, a handful of practical points quietly shape how enjoyable and profitable a villa turns out to be:

  • Access and road quality. A villa at the end of a steep or narrow lane can be a challenge for delivery vehicles, taxis and elderly guests.
  • Water and drainage. Ask how the property is supplied with water and how rain runs off the plot. These questions matter far more than most buyers expect.
  • Noise and neighbours. Visit at different times of day, including evening and early morning, to hear what the area really sounds like.
  • Internet and mobile coverage. Remote-working guests will notice immediately if connectivity is weak, and it is harder to fix in some hillside locations.
  • Distance to services. Think about how far it is to a supermarket, a pharmacy and a hospital, because these distances matter on the day you need them.

Matching area to purpose

As a rough guide, owners focused on rental income often look first to the north and east, where demand is broad and consistent. Owners seeking a private family retreat tend to gravitate toward Maenam, Choeng Mon or the quieter hills. Owners who plan to live full-time often prefer the calmer coasts, where daily life feels more settled and neighbours are more likely to be long-term residents.

None of these is a rule. Plenty of successful villas sit in unexpected places, and a well-presented, well-managed home can do well almost anywhere on the island. What matters is that the location fits the purpose you actually have, not the one that sounds most impressive.

The short version

Koh Samui offers a surprisingly wide range of settings within a small island, from lively resort beaches to hushed hillside lanes. Decide what the villa is for, who it is for and how much convenience you need, then visit the shortlisted areas in different seasons and at different times of day.
