Temples, waterfalls and viewpoints: the Koh Samui sights worth leaving the villa for

Samui Luxury Stays8 min read

A villa on Koh Samui is hard to leave. Between the pool, the sea view and a chef in the kitchen, many guests plan to do very little. But the island repays a few well-chosen excursions, and the best ones aren't far away: gilded temples, jungle waterfalls, ancient rock formations and quiet corners that most visitors never see.

This guide gathers the island's best-loved places to visit, grouped by what they offer and where they sit. Opening hours, entrance fees and access can change, so check before you go. Our concierge team is happy to arrange a driver, a guide or a private tour.

Temples and spiritual sites

Samui's temples are active places of worship, and they offer some of the island's most striking sights. Dress respectfully: cover your shoulders and knees, and remove your shoes before entering any building. Speak quietly and avoid pointing your feet toward Buddha images.

  • Big Buddha (Wat Phra Yai). The large seated Buddha on a small island linked to the north coast is one of Samui's best-known landmarks, and is visible from the air as you arrive. Visit in the morning or late afternoon for softer light and smaller crowds.
  • Wat Plai Laem. Close to the Big Buddha, this temple sits beside a lake and is known for its large, many-armed Guanyin statue and its colourful, ornate buildings. The two sites are easy to combine in one trip.
  • Wat Khunaram. In the south of the island, this temple is famous for the preserved body of a monk who is said to have meditated at the moment of his passing. It is a fascinating and distinctive sight, best approached with respect.
  • Quieter shrines and pagodas. Smaller temples and viewpoints, such as the chedi on Chom Daow Mountain and the shrine at Laem Sor, reward visitors who like to avoid the crowds.

Waterfalls and nature

The island's interior is surprisingly green and rugged, with jungle, rubber plantations and several waterfalls.

  • Na Muang waterfalls. Two falls in the south of the island, off the ring road. The first is easy to reach, while the second requires a short walk or climb through the forest, so wear sturdy shoes. The falls are most impressive after rain and can be much quieter in the dry months.
  • Secret Buddha Garden. A garden of statues and sculptures tucked in the hills, created by a local fruit farmer. It is peaceful, slightly mysterious and a lovely mid-morning stop, though the access road is steep.
  • Hin Ta and Hin Yai. These two famous rock formations on the south-east coast, near Lamai, have long been a point of local curiosity and humour. They are best seen with a nearby coastal drive and a swim.
  • Hillside viewpoints. Several lookouts around the island give wide views over the coast and the surrounding islands, especially lovely in the early morning.

Ang Thong National Marine Park

If you plan one big excursion, make it this one. Ang Thong is an archipelago of dozens of limestone islands, protected as a national marine park, and often described as one of the most beautiful sights in the Gulf of Thailand. A day trip typically combines a boat journey between the islands, swimming and snorkelling in clear water, kayaking and a walk to a viewpoint.

  • Choose an authorised operator. Reputable operators work within the park rules and take safety seriously.
  • Private or small-group options give more flexibility and fewer crowds than a large tour boat.
  • Check conditions. Trips can be limited or cancelled in rough weather and during the monsoon months, so plan for the drier season where you can.
  • Bring the basics: sunscreen, a hat, a towel, water and a waterproof bag for your phone.
Go early, go with someone local

Most popular sights are far more enjoyable at opening time, before tour buses arrive. A private driver or guide who knows the island can sequence a day so that you are always a step ahead of the crowd.

Beaches worth a visit

Each coast has its own character, and trying more than one is a good idea.

  • Chaweng. The longest and liveliest beach, with water sports, restaurants and nightlife. A good place to see Samui at its busiest.
  • Lamai. Slightly quieter and more laid-back, with a mix of local and visitor life.
  • Choeng Mon. A calm, sheltered bay on the north-east coast, suited to families and relaxed swimming.
  • Lipa Noi. On the west coast, shallow, quiet and wonderful for sunsets.
  • Smaller coves. Several hidden bays, some reached through hotel grounds or a short walk, are excellent for snorkelling.

Markets and culture

Samui's culture shows itself most readily in its markets and everyday life. The Friday walking street in Bophut and the island's night markets, described in our market guide, are excellent places to see local life. Fishing villages, small temples and roadside stalls reward anyone who slows down and looks.

Active days out

For guests who want more energy, the island offers kayaking, diving, snorkelling, stand-up paddleboarding, Thai boxing sessions, jungle zip-lines and golf. Reputable operators, proper safety equipment and honest advice about conditions matter more than the brochure. Our article on experiences guests remember looks at the wider range of activities available.

Visiting responsibly

  • Respect sacred places. Dress modestly, remove shoes, stay quiet and follow local signs.
  • Choose animal experiences with care. Look for sanctuaries that prioritise the animals' welfare and do not offer riding or performances, and avoid anything that seems to keep animals in poor conditions.
  • Protect the sea. Do not touch coral or feed fish, use reef-safe sunscreen where possible and take your rubbish with you.
  • Be careful on the roads. Motorbike accidents are common on the island. If you are not an experienced rider, a driver is a far safer choice.
  • Carry travel insurance, which is essential for any adventurous activity.

Planning your days

A good mix for a week might include one big day, such as Ang Thong, one cultural day combining the Big Buddha, Wat Plai Laem and a market, one nature day with waterfalls and viewpoints and the rest at the villa. Group sights by area to avoid long drives, because the ring road can be slow, particularly around Chaweng. Our sample itineraries show how to combine these into a single, easy day.

The short version

Samui rewards a few outings from the villa: the temples of the north coast, the waterfalls and rock formations of the south, a day at Ang Thong marine park and an evening at a market or on a quiet beach. Dress respectfully at temples, go early, use reputable operators and let someone local do the driving.
