Moving to Koh Samui with children: schools, healthcare and family-friendly areas

Samui Luxury Stays7 min read

Moving to Koh Samui with children is very doable, and many families find the island a wonderful place to grow up. The key is to plan around the things that matter most to families: schools, healthcare, safety, space to play and a home that works day to day. This guide covers what to think about before you choose where to live.

Schools

The island has a range of schools, including international schools, mostly clustered in the north and east. Places can fill up, and entry requirements, fees and term dates differ, so contact schools early. Fees are a significant part of a family budget. Ask each school about transport, the curriculum, class sizes and how they support children arriving mid-year.

Choosing an area as a family

Many families choose the north and north-east, such as Maenam, Bophut and Choeng Mon, because supermarkets, schools, clinics and activities are close. A hillside villa with a view may look appealing, but think about steep roads, the school run and distance from help. See our full area guide for the trade-offs.

Choosing the right home

  • Pools and safety. A private pool is a joy, but needs a fence or cover and constant supervision with small children
  • Stairs and levels. Villas often have several levels, steps and open balconies. Check them with children in mind
  • Outdoor space. Look for a safe garden and shade, and check for hazards
  • Air-conditioning in the bedrooms, since the climate is hot and humid
  • Mosquito screens and pest control, especially in green, quiet areas
  • Distance to a clinic or hospital and to the nearest shops

Healthcare

The island has private hospitals and clinics with English-speaking staff, and standards are generally high. Choose international health insurance that clearly covers children, check what it covers on the island, and register with a clinic or paediatrician early so you know where to go when you need to.

Schools on Koh Samui

Families can choose between British-style international schools, a bilingual Thai-English school and a French school. This is a general list to start your research, not a recommendation.

  • International School of Samui (North-east, between Bophut and Chaweng): British curriculum with Cambridge IGCSE and A-Levels. English, with other languages taught. Ages 3 to 18.
  • PanyaDee, The British International School of Samui (Bophut area): Cambridge and IGCSE programme taught in English, with Thai as a subject. Nursery to Year 13.
  • Oonrak Koh Samui School (bilingual) (North coast, Maenam area): Thai National Curriculum with British and Cambridge content. About half the lessons in English and half in Thai. Pre-Kindergarten to Primary 6.
  • Lamai International School (Lamai / Maret): British and Cambridge curriculum for younger children. Foundation to Year 6.
  • Windfield International School (Lamai / Maret): British and French curricula, with a multilingual approach.
  • Greenacre International School (South of the island (Na Muang)): British EYFS and IGCSE programme taught in English, with Thai also taught. Ages 3 to 16.
  • IFDS – École Française de Koh Samui (Chaweng): French national curriculum, taught in French with English and Spanish. Preschool to final year.
  • Samui Centre of Learning (Chaweng): British curriculum with IGCSE and A-Level courses.

School details change, and directories do not always agree on locations, curricula or fees. Please confirm current details, availability and fees directly with each school before you decide.

Everyday life and the rainy season

Most areas are not walkable, so plan for a car. If you drive, you need the right licence, and children must travel safely. The rainy season typically runs from about October to December. Check roofs, drainage and access roads, and plan indoor activities. Many families find the island’s beaches, markets and outdoor life the best part of the move.

Settling in

  • Visit schools before you commit to an area
  • Join local parent groups to meet other families
  • Rent first, buy later, once you know what suits your family
  • Keep copies of passports, visas, leases and school records together

Plan around schools, healthcare and safety first, then choose an area and a home that suit family life.
